Embedded Labor

Installation / Sculpture · 2026

  • Materials: Ceramic, metal, mixed media

Embedded Labor examines how stability is produced through forms of work that often remain unseen until a system begins to fail. The installation considers pressure not as something carried by a single individual, but as something distributed across bodies, objects, and institutional structures.

Ceramic and metal operate as both material and structural conditions within the work. Their different properties—rigidity, fragility, weight, and resistance—allow pressure to move between parts rather than remain localized. What appears stable is therefore dependent on continuous adjustment and support.

The work developed from my broader research into healthcare systems, where responsibility is distributed among patients, clinicians, families, administrative structures, and physical infrastructure. Rather than representing a hospital literally, Embedded Labor focuses on the mechanisms of holding, transferring, and compensating that allow a system to continue functioning.

The installation asks what kinds of labor become visible only when they are no longer being performed, and how stability can itself be understood as the temporary result of ongoing, uneven maintenance.
